The Growing Problem of Plastic in Our Oceans

Plastic pollution is one of the most serious environmental problems facing the planet today. Every year, around 8 million tonnes of plastic waste enter the ocean from coastal countries. Much of this plastic does not biodegrade; instead it slowly breaks down into tiny fragments known as microplastics.

These microplastics have now been found almost everywhere scientists have looked, from Arctic sea ice to the deepest point of the Mariana Trench. Researchers have also detected microplastics in human blood, raising concerns about possible effects on our health.

Marine animals are hit especially hard. Sea turtles often mistake plastic bags for jellyfish, and studies suggest that more than half of all sea turtles have eaten plastic at some point in their lives. Seabirds are affected too, with plastic found in the stomachs of around 90% of seabird species.

In my opinion, governments are not doing enough. Banning single-use plastic bags is a good start, but it is not nearly enough on its own. We all need to change our habits, and companies must take responsibility for the packaging they produce.

In conclusion, ocean plastic is a crisis that affects wildlife, ecosystems and potentially our own health. If nothing changes, some estimates suggest there could be more plastic than fish in the ocean by weight by 2050.
